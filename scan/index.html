The Headline

Google already puts you first for cupcakes in Merrillville and Crown Point, and your jar is a product most bakeries would envy. What’s leaking is everything after the first click: a 4.1-star rating with nothing asking happy customers to review, a flagship page that scores 15 on a phone, event leads that can arrive with no contact details, and an email list nobody can join.

01What we found

4.1 stars: the lowest rating among the bakeries Google ranks around you.

You have 262 Google reviews and you are the first map result for “cupcakes Merrillville”, “bakery Merrillville” and even “cupcakes Crown Point”. But the bakeries Google lists beside you sit at 4.3 to 4.8: Great Harvest 4.8, Nothing Bundt Cakes 4.8, The Peach Cobbler Factory 4.6, Marilyn’s 4.5. The newest review Google returned is a one-star from May about a birthday cake that fell over before it got home, and a phone call that ended with “nothing they can do”. Nothing asks happy customers for a review after they buy, and your website shows no reviews at all.

Your flagship jar page scores 15 out of 100 on a phone.

The 8 oz Cupcake in a Jar page (its address is /products/demo-1) takes 6.9 seconds to show its main image on a mobile connection, freezes for 2.0 seconds while scripts load, and its layout jumps as the bundle builder arrives (layout shift 0.869; Google treats anything above 0.25 as poor). Share that link and Google, Facebook and iMessage preview it with an app’s admin note: “Your Bundle is Unlisted… Do Not Delete.” And a shopper cannot buy one 8 oz jar; the smallest option is a box of two.

Event bookings can arrive with no way to reply, and replies are promised in 24 to 48 hours.

On both booking forms, for the truck and for the dessert cart, the name, email, phone and event date are all optional. Only the time, headcount, address and details are required. The contact page promises a reply within 24 to 48 hours, and there is no chat on any page. An event planner comparing three caterers usually books whoever answers first.

Your email tool is installed, but nobody can join the list.

Klaviyo loads on every page, yet no page carries a sign-up form or pop-up, so the list only grows from people who have already bought. Google Analytics 4 and Google Ads are counting, which is good. No Meta pixel fired on a full visit to the homepage, so the 8,700 people who follow you on Facebook and the 4,500 on Instagram cannot be shown an ad for what they looked at.

The truck and the machines barely exist online.

Search Google Maps for “food truck Merrillville” and Cutie the Cupcake Truck is not among the 12 results; it has no listing of its own. The vending machines, which you won a pitch competition to fund, are mentioned once on the whole site, in one sentence on the About page. There is no page that says where a machine is. The Truck and Dessert Cart pages each carry a large blank space where a private video will not play.

Google says you close at 6. Your website says 7.

Your Google listing shows Tuesday to Saturday, 10 AM to 6 PM. The website’s contact page says 10 AM to 7 PM. The site shows (219) 472-0858, while the store’s policy page and your sell sheet also list 219-712-6436. A store that ships food nationwide has no refund policy and no terms of service published.

06Three fixes for this week
1

Fix the Google listing in fifteen minutes. Match the hours to the shop, reply to the May review with what you’d do next time, and start asking every happy customer for a review at the counter and in the order email.

2

Stop losing leads in twenty minutes. Make name, email, phone and date required on both booking forms, and replace the admin note on the 8 oz jar and Signature Cupcakes pages with a real description.

3

Let people join your list. Klaviyo is already paid for: switch on a sign-up form, and connect Shopify’s Facebook & Instagram channel so your followers can be reached again.
